Research Overview

My research lies at the intersection of probability theory, stochastic optimization, machine learning theory, and computational mathematics. I am particularly interested in understanding the mathematical foundations of stochastic learning algorithms, with an emphasis on algorithmic stability, generalization, and heavy-tailed phenomena.

I earned my Ph.D. in Financial Mathematics from Florida State University under the supervision of Dr. Lingjiong Zhu . My doctoral research focused on the algorithmic stability and generalization of heavy-tailed stochastic gradient algorithms, particularly stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGDm).

My broader research interests include stochastic differential equations, decentralized optimization, stochastic sampling methods, scientific machine learning, data assimilation, and applications of mathematics to financial modeling and risk management.

Studying the emergence of heavy-tailed behavior in decentralized stochastic gradient descent algorithms, particularly through homogenized stochastic models. Investigating penalized fractional underdamped Langevin dynamics and their potential applications to constrained stochastic sampling and optimization. The project explores the interplay between heavy-tailed stochastic noise, momentum, and penalization mechanisms.
